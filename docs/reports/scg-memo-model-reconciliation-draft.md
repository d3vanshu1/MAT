# Memo–Model Reconciliation — SCG

**24 August 2026**

This report compares the numeric claims made in SCG's IC memos against the figures in the financial model, and reports where the two disagree.

---

## 1. What we found

### The memo's FY26 revenue is £9.6m above the model

The 2nd IC Memo of 18 May states SCG will deliver **£194m of revenue** in FY Mar-26. The financial model shows **£184.4m** — a gap of **£9.6m, or 5.2%**.

> "SCG is expected to deliver £194m revenue and £57m cash EBITDA for FY Mar-26"
> — 2nd IC Memo, Executive Summary

The same £194m figure appears twice more in the memo, on the Scorecard page and in the LfL Revenue Evolution chart.

The model figure is Total Group revenue on the FS Summary tab of *Project Saint — Financial model_vS, updated for current trading to Mar-26*.

FY Mar-26 is the entry year, and this figure anchors the entry multiple. The £57m cash EBITDA quoted in the same sentence sits within about £33,000 of the model's Adjusted EBITDA for that year (£57.03m) — so the revenue figure is the outlier, not the pair.

*We compare figures only. Whether the memo or the model is correct is a question for the deal team.*

---

## 2. What this covers

We read four IC memos — the screening memo, the second and third IC memos, and the June update — and compared the numbers in them against SCG's financial model and the vendor financial diligence report. The memos contain 313 numeric claims between them. Of those, 87 stated an operating figure for a named period that the model also carries, which is what makes a direct comparison possible. One of those 87 disagreed with the model by enough to report, and it is the finding above. The remaining 226 claims were not comparable, for the reasons set out below.

---

## 3. What we could not check

**Claims about deal structure, valuation and returns — 151.** Entry and exit multiples, IRR and money-multiple projections, and fee and financing mechanics. The operating model holds no counterpart to these by construction, so there is nothing to compare them against.

**Claims stating a sensitivity rather than a base case — 41.** Where the memo quotes an upside or downside case, there is no single base-case figure in the model to test it against.

**Claims stated as rates, margins or conversion ratios — 45.** Growth rates, margin percentages and conversion ratios. **The model holds the underlying figures these are built from — revenue and gross profit by year — but the tool does not yet compute rates from those levels, so it cannot compare them.** This is the largest remaining gap, and closing it is the main thing the next version adds.

**Claims with no comparable figure in the model — 27.** Operating figures stated as absolute amounts where the model carries no matching line for that period.

A further 14 claims matched the model on both metric and period but described a different part of the business than the model line they landed on. These are recorded and available, but never reported as disagreements — a difference in scope is not evidence of an error. Of the remaining claims, 21 restated figures already counted elsewhere, and 13 gave no period, which leaves nothing to match on.

---

## 4. Method

A disagreement is reported only where a memo claim and a model figure describe the same metric, the same period and the same part of the business. Both are converted to the same units, and the difference between them is calculated arithmetically — never inferred or estimated. A difference is reported only if it is large enough on both measures at once: at least £2m in absolute terms and at least 5% in relative terms. Where the tool cannot establish that two figures are genuinely comparable — different scope, no stated period, incompatible units, or a model reference that resolves to several conflicting figures — it declines to assert a disagreement rather than guess at one. That is why the number of findings here is small: what is reported has been verified, and what could not be verified has been withheld rather than presented as a likely problem.
